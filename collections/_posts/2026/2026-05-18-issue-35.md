It's been a fun old week recently. I am working on lots of things at once and have been more productive than I have been in a while, which is fantastic.

Last week I released a whole new Brush Ninja app, a [Stop Motion Animator](https://brush.ninja/create/stop-motion-animation/)!

It's been on my wish list for a while, and this is an early version, but it's already a lot of fun to use. It was actually surprisingly easy to make. I've been delaying it for a long time because I thought it would be as complex as the main Brush Ninja app, but it was actually much simpler. As such, we have a minimal version 1 released for everyone to use! As normal, it's free and I'd love to know what you think. I've already been working on updates to it, which will be coming out soon.

Talking of the [Pixel Camera App](https://brush.ninja/create/pixel-camera/), I've been working on that as well. It's not ready yet, but I'm adding a new mode that lets you record short videos and save them as pixelated animated GIFs!

Finally, I've started planning a sister app to the [Stop Motion Animator](https://brush.ninja/create/stop-motion-animation/) that will be a Timelapse Animator. It will share a lot of the interface and background code with the Stop Motion Animator, but will be designed for taking photos at regular intervals and then stitching them together into a timelapse animated GIF. I'm really excited about this one as I think it will be a lot of fun to use, and will allow people to create some really interesting content. It'll probably be a few weeks before it's ready to release, but I'm looking forward to sharing it when it's ready.

## GB Studio Music

I've mentioned [GB Studio]({% link _library/app/2025-08-25-gameboy-studio.md %}) before. It's a fantastic free, open source game engine that lets you make games for the Game Boy. It's a really fun, simple way to build games with a minimal interface, and it's a great way to get into game dev.

Recently, the main developer has been creating a new tool for making music for GB Studio games, and it runs in a browser, which I find fascinating. It doesn't really have a name, but I'll call it [Gameboy Studio Music][gbstudio-music-link] for now.

{% include embed-image.html image="/images/updates/2026/gb-studio-music-1.png" alt="Gameboy Studio Music Menu" %}

I find it fascinating because 1) it's a really nice music tool and 2) it runs in the browser and it's going to be added to the main app as well, which suggests the rest of the app could run in a web browser too. As a developer who makes web apps, I really want to see this happen. Fingers crossed!

If you want to try it out, then I recommend opening up the example songs and playing with them. The interface is quite simple but, if you haven't seen this sort of thing before, it could be a little overwhelming at first.

{% include embed-image.html image="/images/updates/2026/gb-studio-music-2.png" alt="Gameboy Studio Music Editing Screen" %}

I've seen and played with these sorts of things before, but I don't have a musical bone in my body, so creating a song from scratch is almost impossible for me. However, I have been playing with the example songs and tweaking them to see how they work, and it's really fun.

I actually want to make an app like this, albeit much simpler, for [BeepMini](https://beepmini.com), my web-based game-making app. This has definitely inspired me to pull my finger out and get on with it. Especially since the current ["music editor"](https://beepmini.com/tools/beepmini-music-editor/) is just a textarea you have to manually edit, which is the least user-friendly thing I could have made 😬.

Anyway, check it out, and if you like the idea of game dev, you could do a lot worse than trying GB Studio whilst you're at it.

**[Try GB Studio Music][gbstudio-music-link]**

## Wildwood Teaser Trailer

{% include embed-youtube.html video="POneS8h1jyU" %}

Laika have released a teaser trailer for their new film Wildwood. Their films are always a treat, and this one looks like they've really gone all out. Along with Aardman, Laika are one of the few studios still making stop motion films. It must be a really difficult thing to justify given the hit-based nature of the film industry, but I am really pleased they keep on doing it!

As much as I love 3D animation, there's something really special about stop motion (I even made a stop motion app. Have I mentioned that?). Wildwood is an original IP based on a [2011 book of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildwood_(novel)) that I haven't read, but based on the trailer it looks like a really fun fantasy adventure story, which is just what I enjoy.

The fact it's handcrafted by real artists gives it a unique personality that is harder to achieve with 3D, and non-existent with AI animation. I can't wait to see the full film when it comes out.

If you like the look of this, then the [Laika channel](https://www.youtube.com/@LAIKAStudios) has a bunch of [behind-the-scenes videos](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLALN6nqI7sQLqIySvNwUoN1LEaEcHqycv) showing just what goes into making a film like this. ## Sparked Discoveries

Here’s a handful of links I found intriguing recently:

## [Budapest Courtyards (2014-2016)](https://marchandmeffre.com/budapest)

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://marchandmeffre.com/budapest" %}

This is a beautiful collection of photos of the courtyards of Budapest. The photographer, Marchand Meffre, has a really interesting style and has captured some really beautiful images. ## [Constellation Draw](https://neal.fun/constellation-draw/)

{% include embed-image.html image="/images/updates/2026/constellation-draw.webp" alt="Constellation Draw" %}

I know I talk about everything Neal does, but it's always fun and unusual. This is a fun little game that I totally missed when it came out. You can draw your own constellation and then see how it would look in the night sky. Classic Neal. It's simple to use and fun to waste half an hour playing with it.

## [100 Greatest Novels](https://www.theguardian.com/books/ng-interactive/2026/may/12/the-100-best-novels-of-all-time)

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://www.theguardian.com/books/ng-interactive/2026/may/12/the-100-best-novels-of-all-time" %}

The Guardian newspaper have published a list of the "100 Greatest Novels of All Time", which is obviously a very subjective thing, but it's interesting to see what they chose. They do have information on how the list was decided. Apparently, it involved votes from authors, critics, and academics worldwide. I read a lot of novels but tend not to read the classics or other more "high-brow" content, so I thought it would be interesting to see what they chose. Of the 100 novels, I've read one, which was George Orwell's *1984*. I have heard of many of the others, but they don't excite me. Let me know if you think I might be missing out, though!
