## [Wikitrivia](https://wikitrivia.tomjwatson.com/daily)

<!-- {% include embed-image.html image="/images/updates/2026/happiness-map.png" alt="A zoomed out view of the happiness map" %} -->

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://wikitrivia.tomjwatson.com/daily" %}

I really enjoy this game, it reminds me of Play Your Cards Right. The game is simple, you are given a starting point in history and the year it happened. Then you are given a series of events and you have to place them in the timeline in the correct order. You don't need to know the exact year, just if it's higher or lower than the other things you've placed. This felt really low pressure and I enjoyed getting things in the right order. Plus it didn't make me feel as stupid as some of the other games, even when I got things wrong the distance between the positions was often small so I felt like I was learning things rather than being bad at history.

## [TimeGuessr](https://timeguessr.com)

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://timeguessr.com" %}

TimeGuessr is a bit like Anthropeum, you have to guess the year and location a photo was taken. The photos vary in age quite a lot, and some of them are very recent. I'm writing this on Sunday 26th July and the first photo in the daily challenge is of Jo Biden - although I'm not sure where it was taken.

## [I Love Hue](https://plays.org/i-love-hue/)

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://plays.org/i-love-hue/" %}

This is the sort of browser based game we might have put on Miniclip 20 years ago. It's a simple game where you have to re-arrange a grid of colored tiles to make a gradient fit. It's a simple concept which I found surprisingly enjoyable, especially considering my colour blindness. I was still able to play it quite successfully, although there were a few points where I couldn't see the difference between some of the colours, but fortunately looking at the brightness of the tiles was often enough to work around these issues.

## [Sporcle](https://www.sporcle.com/)

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://www.sporcle.com/" %}

Sporcle is a website that has a huge collection of quizzes and games. There are quizzes on all sorts of topics, from geography to history to pop culture. That's it really. The quizzes are technically quite simple, but also quite addictive.

## [Geohistory](https://geohistory.gg/)

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://geohistory.gg/" %}

Give a man a globe and a pin and you'll keep him happy for hours. Geohistory was actually suggested to me by Alex, the same friend who mentioned Anthropeum a couple of issues ago. Geohistory is a game that tests your knowledge of geography and history by asking you to place historical events on a world map. I found it to have an American slant, but it does cover world history. I also found it to be more tricky than some of the other games, although some of the things were very straight forward. Plus the interface is a bit frustrating, if you change tabs to look something up it often resets to an earlier question changing your score when you either get it very wrong because you think you're answering the next question, or you get it right because you now know the answer to the previous question. Hopefully they can address this since the game can be a lot of fun.

## Free Education

I recently discovered that some US universities have made the course materials for their classes available online for free. Obviously this is not quite the same as a full education, you don't get the tutors, feedback, peer support, or the eventual qualification, but you do get access to the course materials and you can work through them at your own pace.

This is great for people who want to learn something new without the pressure of exams or the expense of a full education.

* [https://ocw.mit.edu/](https://ocw.mit.edu/) - MIT OpenCourseWare (OCW) is a web-based publication of virtually all MIT course content. OCW is open and available to the world and is a permanent MIT activity.
* [https://learn.saylor.org/](https://learn.saylor.org/) - Saylor Academy offers free online courses at the college and professional levels.
* [https://openstax.org/](https://openstax.org/) - OpenStax is part of Rice University and provides free, peer-reviewed, openly licensed textbooks that are available in free digital formats and for a low cost in print.

Finally a possible bonus option that you may already know about is [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/). This is a non-profit educational organization that provides free online educational resources. It covers a wide range of subjects, including math, science, economics, history, and more. The platform offers instructional videos, practice exercises, and a personalized learning dashboard that allows learners to study at their own pace.
