## [Ian's Shoelace Site](https://www.fieggen.com/shoelace/secureknot.htm)

Firstly - the secure knot is a great idea, and I'm surprised this is the first time I've seen it. I was in the Scouts as a kid, and know a lot of different knots, but the standard shoelace knot is what I was always taught. I will definitely be trying this as it looks really useful. However, I found out about this site because of this article on [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/ians-shoelace-site-is-still-the-best-destination-for-tying-your-shoes/).

Aftermath is a site covering videogames and the internet and in the article they talk about not only how to tie shoelaces, but also why sites that hyperfocus on one thing, are slowly disappearing from the web. Sites that are passion projects, or quirky fun niches. Having used the internet since the mid 90s I have seen many of these sites, in the early days it's all there was. And I really like this sort of thing, it's what NinjaSparks is all about. However it's also sad to see them being lost. The problem is that most people don't really use the internet any more. People focus on a few social networks, and maybe a news site or two. Google is slowly killing their search engine, filling it with inaccurate ai, and adverts. People are turning to AI like ChatGPT for ansewrs, which is also often wrong, and doesn't actually send people to the original sources. This is one of the reasons my websites focus on interactive content. Apps and games can not easily be replaced by AI, and people still have to visit my site to use them. But I worry that the internet is becoming a bland, homogenised place and perhaps quirky niche sites like Ian's Shoelace site will become a thing of the past.

## How Animators Created Toy Story 5

{% include embed-youtube.html video="mfYfkmDwPVM" %}

This videa is a behind-the-scenes look at how the animators created Toy Story 5. It compares the process now with the process used in the original film. It's a great insight into the work that goes into making an animated film, and shows just how much things have changed in the 30 year gap. If you're interested then [Blender]({% link _library/app/2025-03-20-blender.md %}) is a great free tool to get started with 3D animation, and there are a lot of tutorials online to help you get started.

As an aside, if you want to have a career in animation then the channel that posted this, [ClassCreatives](https://www.youtube.com/@ClassCreatives/videos), is a really good start. I also recommend the Pixar in a Box course on [Khan Academy](https://www.khanacademy.org/partner-content/pixar).

## Sparked Discoveries

Here’s a handful of links I found intriguing recently:

## [Animation Hub](https://animationhub.eu/)

I mentioned student animations above, and this website is full of them. Animation Hub is a collection of student animations from around the world. Keep in mind that some of them have mature themes, despite being animations, may not be suitable for children. But there's a lot of awesome work here, and it's a great place to find inspiration or just to find some joy.

## [Anthropeum](https://anthropeum.com/)

Anthropeum is a historical game recommended to me by a friend. You are shown a series of historical artifacts from around the world and you have to guess where and when they are from. Once you've guessed you are given a bit more information and context about the artifact, which makes it both educational and engaging. You are also given a score based upon how accurate your guesses are, and you can see where your score compares globally. I've only played for a couple of days but it's a fun way to learn about history and the world - I'm already improving my knowledge of the locations of countries.


## [Play Geography](https://www.playgeography.com/)

Talking of geographic knowledge, this is a good one for learning the locations of countries. The games are simple and you run it in your browser. You just have to find the requested countries on a map. You can do the whole world, or specific continents. There's also games for learning US States, flags (I have [a flag game](https://brush.ninja/play/country-flags/) too), capitals, and more. It's a good way to learn geography in a fun way.


## [Hostile Volume](https://hostilevolume.com/)

This is a fun little game that challenges your computer skills and patience. The goal is simple, to set the volume of your computer to a specific level using the widget on the screen. It starts easy, but quickly gets out of have until you rage quit. Most of the solutions are obvious, but performing them is not!
