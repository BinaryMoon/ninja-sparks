## KEY APP

{% include embed-youtube.html video="Is8N7B9b0GQ" %}

This is beautiful. It's the story of an 84 year old man who has been designing a detailed world since he was small. He draws maps elements on sheets of paper and when laid out they form a huge make believe world with a rich history.

To decide how the world evolves, he has made a simple card game using a customised deck of playing cards (it's actually about 100 cards). These cards tell him what to draw, edit, change, grow, or improve. I find the whole thing fascinating.

In fact the whole project boils down to processes and systems. Which really calls to me. A lot of the things I do these days are system based, trying to make things that react to each other in interesting ways. Or making tools that improve processes. I find it really interesting to see how other people do this, and how they use systems to create things - especially when it's so creative.

The world has a story and a history. The map has evolved over time and cities and towns have come and gone. Rivers have changed course, and the world has been shaped by the people who live in it. It's a really lovely example of creativity and how it can be driven by systems and processes. It feels not dissimilar to a town building game like Sim City, but considerably more organic, and without a loss condition. It's a world that exists and evolves like our own.

The documentary film team, who make a lot of content about games of all types, found out about this and made a short documentary about it. Even better, they were able to spend 3 days with him laying out this world so he could see it in its entirety for the first time in 14 years! It's a lovely story about creativity and the pleasure of making things for the sake of it, rather than for profit or an audience.

I find it really inspiring and it reminds of why I keep making things. It's because I want the thing I am making to exist. Unfortunately I also need to pay the bills so I always have this thing in the back of my mind about how I will make money from the project, and I wonder how much this affects what and how I make.

I would love the freedom to just make things for the joy of making them.

## Sparked Discoveries

Here’s a handful of links I found intriguing recently:

### [Quentin Blake Centre for Illustration](https://qbcentre.org.uk/)

{% include embed-browsershots.html url="https://qbcentre.org.uk/" %}

A new museum in London decdicated to the work of Quentin Blake, the beloved illustrator known for his collaborations with Roald Dahl. I grew up with Roald Dahl's books and Quentin Blake's illustrations, so this is a lovely tribute to their work and I'm looking forward to visiting it.


### [Elias Thorne's Chatbots and LLMs](https://www.404media.co/elias-thorne-chatbots-llms-chatgpt-lighthouse-keeper-story/)

{% include embed-image.html image="/images/updates/2026/elias-thorne.jpg" alt="The back of the head of a man looking over the sea" %}

This amused me when I read it, but then I tested it and, well, it is spot on. The article talks about how chatbots and LLMs (like ChatGPT) often use the same small pool of names and themes when you ask it to tell a story. I even tried it myself and it wrote a story about a lady called Eliza and her grandfather, I asked it what the grandfather's name was is it suggested Elias. I feel very conflicted by AI, the environmental harms are real, but also I feel like there are genuine uses to it. But anyone actually using it for creative work (story writing, image and video creation), is missing the point of creativity. I use it for things like proofreading but I don't want it to be doing the fun creative work for me. I'm currently looking at local AI tools that run on my computer so I can rely less on big tech.

### [Hallucinate](https://hallucinate.site/)

{% include embed-image.html image="/images/updates/2026/hallucinate.png" caption="Dancing at a virtual rave" %}

I've never been into clubbing, but this is a fun little browser based world where you can dance with others whilst watching live music. It's a virtual festival. There's controls scattered all over the screen for different movements and customizations. There's a main stage, but there's also other smaller stages, and a chill out area. Keep in mind though that my computer is quite powerful and I rarely hear the fans turn on, but every time I've tried this my computer has worked really hard and the fans have powered up.
