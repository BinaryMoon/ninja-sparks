## Favorite Pico-8 Games

## Sebastians Quest

Adam Atomic has made lots of little games over the years, and [Sebastians Quest](https://adamatomic.itch.io/sebastians-quest) is one of my favorites. It's a very simple game, where you play as a pug, named Sebastian, who wants to eat the cheese. But to do this he has to find his way through a maze. This was the first of Adams games I really got into, and it had lots of lovely "ah-ha" moments where I learnt what was happening or discovered something new, and this is the sort of thing I love about games. I want to try to add these moments into my own projects, but it's hard to get right. Like all of these games I don't want to say too much so I don't spoil the fun, but I really enjoyed this and have played it through a few times.

It's free to play on [Itch.io](https://adamatomic.itch.io/sebastians-quest).

## Moss Moss

## Snakey game I don't remember the name of

I would have said that Sebastians Quest was my favourite Pico8 game but then I played Moss Moss. I mentioned above how I enjoy the "ah-ha" moments in games, and Moss Moss is full of them. The first time round might take you an hour or so to finish, but once you've finished the game you realise you can do it a bit quicker. My best was about 35 seconds - quite a change - and I'm not going to tell you how! There's a lot of really satisfying moments in Moss Moss and very few times where I got stuck or felt frustrated.

### Recent N64 Game Releases

{% include embed-youtube.html video="_yFPb5NH0Gc" %}

[Watch the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_yFPb5NH0Gc)

The N64 stopped being made in 2002, but people are still releasing new games for it.

This video covers some recent releases, and I'm amazed not only that people are still making them, but that some of them look genuinely fun to play.

The N64 is one of my favourite consoles. It came out when I was a teenager, and I have very fond memories of playing it. I certainly didn't imagine that nearly 30 years later people would still be figuring out new things to make with it.

Perhaps that's the thing I like most about all these projects. Creativity doesn't always need more powerful hardware, bigger worlds or fewer restrictions. Sometimes giving yourself a smaller box to work inside gives you more reason to find something interesting to do with it.
